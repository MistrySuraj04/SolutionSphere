# Question 29: Top Earners

**Platform:** HackerRank

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Problem Statement**

We define an employee's total earnings to be their monthly  worked,
and the maximum total earnings to be the maximum total earnings for any employee in the Employee table. Write a query to find the maximum total earnings for all employees as well as the total number of employees who have maximum total earnings.
Then print these values as  space-separated integers.

### Solution

SELECT TOP 1 SALARY * MONTHS, COUNT(DISTINCT EMPLOYEE_ID)
FROM EMPLOYEE
GROUP BY SALARY * MONTHS
ORDER BY SALARY * MONTHS DESC;

### Explanation

### **Purpose**:
The query identifies the highest **total earnings** (calculated as `SALARY * MONTHS`) from the `EMPLOYEE` table and counts how many unique employees have that total earnings value.

---

### **Query Breakdown**:
1. **Selected Columns**:
   - **`SALARY * MONTHS`**:
     - Calculates the total earnings for each employee by multiplying their **SALARY** by **MONTHS**.

   - **`COUNT(DISTINCT EMPLOYEE_ID)`**:
     - Counts the number of unique employees who have the same total earnings (`SALARY * MONTHS`).

---

2. **Data Source**:
   - **`FROM EMPLOYEE`**: Specifies the **EMPLOYEE** table as the source of the data.

---

3. **Grouping**:
   - **`GROUP BY SALARY * MONTHS`**:
     - Groups the records based on the value of **SALARY * MONTHS**.
     - Each unique total earnings value is treated as a group, and the count of unique employees in each group is calculated.

---

4. **Ordering**:
   - **`ORDER BY SALARY * MONTHS DESC`**:
     - Sorts the grouped total earnings in descending order (highest total earnings first).

---

5. **Limiting Results**:
   - **`SELECT TOP 1`**:
     - Returns only the **top 1 result** — the group with the highest **SALARY * MONTHS** value.

---

### **Example Walkthrough**
| **EMPLOYEE_ID** | **SALARY** | **MONTHS** | **SALARY * MONTHS** |
|-----------------|------------|------------|---------------------|
| 1               | 1000       | 12         | 12000               |
| 2               | 2000       | 12         | 24000               |
| 3               | 3000       | 12         | 36000               |
| 4               | 2000       | 12         | 24000               |

**Step-by-step process**:
- Calculate **SALARY * MONTHS** for each employee.
- Group by **SALARY * MONTHS**, so the unique groups will be:
  - **12000**: 1 employee
  - **24000**: 2 employees (EMPLOYEE_ID 2 and 4)
  - **36000**: 1 employee
- Order by **SALARY * MONTHS DESC**:
  - **36000** (1 employee)
  - **24000** (2 employees)
  - **12000** (1 employee)
- Return only the first row (top 1).

---

### **Result**:
The result will be a single row with two values:
1. **The highest total earnings** (`SALARY * MONTHS`).
2. **The number of distinct employees** who have this highest total earnings.

For the above example, the result would be:
| **SALARY * MONTHS** | **COUNT(DISTINCT EMPLOYEE_ID)** |
|---------------------|---------------------------------|
| **36000**            | **1**                          |
